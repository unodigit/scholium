Why Scholium

Today, the scientific record lives in someone's database. If that operator changes its policy, runs out of money or makes a mistake, the record changes with it. Scholium signs every paper, version and review where it is written, so anyone can check the record, and any faithful copy can prove it is the original.

Open review

Peer review, out in the open.

Formal reviews sit in the margin beside the paper, scored against a public rubric. Anyone can read them. Reviewers can stay anonymous to the authors and still be held to account.

Structured, not starred.

Six criteria, a confidence level and a recommendation. A formal review that is only free text is sent back to its author.

AI pre-review

A careful first read, within about an hour.

Soon after a paper is listed, a team of AI agents reads it and files an advisory report. Each finding links to the exact passage, so authors and reviewers can judge it for themselves.

  • Catches the tedious things. References that don't resolve, claims without evidence, numbers that don't add up.
  • Open to challenge. Authors can rebut a finding. Reviewers can confirm it or mark it wrong.
  • Never a verdict. It is labelled advisory everywhere, and it never changes a paper's status.

How it works

From draft to discussed, in four steps.

    Sign in

  2. 2

    Submit

    Upload your PDF or LaTeX. Your browser fingerprints and signs it, and your co-authors add their consent.

    Get listed

    A volunteer moderator checks it against public policy, aiming to decide within 48 hours. Every decision is a signed record.

Principles

Four things Scholium won't do.

No token.

There is nothing to trade or speculate on, and no token-weighted votes on scientific questions. Ever.

No paywall.

Free to read and free to publish, with an open licence by default.

No verdicts.

Scholium isn't a journal. It lists, reviews and badges papers. It doesn't accept or reject science.

No quiet edits.

Corrections, retractions and moderation decisions are signed, public records. Old versions stay readable.

What is Scholium?

Scholium is a free preprint and open peer-review network. Authors publish papers and new versions, signed on their own device. Reviewers post structured, signed reviews beside the text. Readers can check every record's signature and history themselves, in the browser or offline.

How is Scholium different from arXiv?

Scholium works alongside arXiv rather than replacing it, and you can import a paper from arXiv and claim it with your signature. What Scholium adds is an author signature on every version and review, structured peer review in the open beside the paper, and archives anyone can verify without trusting the operator.

Is Scholium free?

Yes. Reading and publishing are free, and papers use the CC BY 4.0 licence by default. There is no token, no paywall and no submission fee.

How does Scholium prove who wrote a paper?

When you submit, your browser computes a content fingerprint for each file and signs the version with a signing key that lives on your device and is unlocked by your passkey. Scholium never sees the key. Anyone can check the signature and the chain of versions on the paper's Proof tab, or offline with the scholium verify command.

Can reviewers stay anonymous?

Yes, and they stay accountable. Each person can post one formal review per paper, co-authors and conflicts of interest are refused, and reports stay sealed until two reviewers have committed theirs or 21 days have passed.

What is the AI pre-review?

An advisory report from a team of AI agents, aimed to arrive within about an hour of listing. It flags problems such as references that do not resolve and claims without evidence, each linked to the exact passage. It is labelled "Advisory. Generated by AI agents. Not a peer review." and it never changes a paper's status.

Does Scholium use a blockchain or a cryptocurrency?

There is no cryptocurrency or token, and none is planned. Records are signed and content-addressed with standard cryptography. Public timestamp anchoring is planned for a later phase, and personal data and paper content will never be stored on a blockchain.

What happens if I lose my device?

You choose guardians when you set up your account. If you lose your device, you start recovery with your ORCID iD, three of your five guardians approve it, and after a 72-hour safety window, which you can cancel, your new passkey takes over.

Who decides what gets listed?

Volunteer moderators with subject expertise. They check each submission against a published policy and aim to decide within 48 hours. Every decision names a policy clause and a reason, and is published as a signed record.

Can I take my papers elsewhere?

Yes. Any paper can be exported, with every version, signature and review, as one archive that verifies offline. Citations export to BibTeX and RIS.

What does the name mean?

A scholium is an explanatory note written in the margin of a scholarly text. Scholium keeps a paper and its margin together: reviews, notes and corrections stay beside the text they are about.
